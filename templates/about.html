About the project

How it works

From surplus to a delivered meal in four steps

Every account is approved by an admin, and every listing is matched by area with timers so food doesn't sit around.

1

List

A donor lists surplus food with the pickup area and an expiry time.

2

Offer

The nearest pantries get a time-limited offer. If nobody accepts, it moves to the next nearest, then opens to all.

3

Collect

Drivers with a saved area and address are offered the pickup, nearest first.

4

Deliver & report

Everyone is notified at each step, and partnership reports show what was delivered.

Good to know

Frequently asked questions

Who can use FoodBridge?

Food donors, community pantries and volunteer drivers. Every new account is reviewed and approved by an admin before it can use the portal.

Why do drivers need to add an address?

We rank pickups by distance from the driver's area and offer jobs to the nearest drivers first. Until a driver has saved both an area and a street address in their profile, they can't accept any pickup.

What happens if nobody accepts an offer in time?

Offers have a timer. If it runs out, the next nearest pantry or driver is offered the job. After a few rounds the listing opens to every eligible partner so nothing is left waiting.

What happens when food reaches its expiry time?

An unclaimed or uncollected listing closes automatically and the donor is told. Deliveries already on the road aren't cancelled mid-journey.

Can a listing be cancelled?

Yes. Donors can cancel with a reason, pantries can release a claim, and drivers can hand a job back. Everyone involved is told why.

How are meals and CO₂ savings estimated?

We use about 2 meals per kg of food, which matches OzHarvest's published 2025 figures (17,500 tonnes and 35 million meals), and 1 kg of CO₂-e avoided per kg rescued. They're estimates for reporting, not measurements.
